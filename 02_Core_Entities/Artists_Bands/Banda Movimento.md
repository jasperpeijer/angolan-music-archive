## 1. Biography

### Origins & Formative Years
In the history of Lusophone-African music, Banda Movimento has played an important role in preserving and sharing Angola's national rhythms. Founded in the late 1990s, the band has spent over two decades performing and documenting the country's acoustic musical heritage. Rather than acting strictly as a commercial group, Banda Movimento operates more like a cultural institution focused on keeping traditional Angolan styles—such as semba, rebita, and cabetula—alive. Through their strong musicianship and their official connection to the national broadcaster, they have helped shape the sound of post-colonial and post-conflict Angola.

Banda Movimento was formed on March 2, 1999, during a complex political and social period in Angola's history. At the time, the country was entering the final years of a decades-long civil war. In the midst of this conflict, the Angolan government, led by the MPLA, actively sought to promote national unity and reinforce a shared cultural identity as a stabilizing tool.

### Peak Era
During the 2000s, nearly every major traditional Semba artist who performed on national television or radio was backed by Banda Movimento.

### Political Context
The band's existence is deeply intertwined with post-civil war state-building. Rádio Nacional de Angola is the state broadcaster, controlled by the MPLA government. Following the brutal civil war (which ended in 2002), the state heavily promoted Semba music as the unifying, authentic "Angolan" cultural product to build a cohesive national identity.

Because Banda Movimento was literally on the state payroll via RNA, their music and their backing of classic artists was an extension of this political alignment. They were the musical wing of a state-funded effort to centralize Angolan culture around Luandan Semba and Kimbundu proverbs.
