# [[Milá Melo]]

> **Core Summary:** Milá Melo was an Angolan singer active primarily in the 1970s. While extensive biographical details about her early life are scarce in available public records, her musical footprint is documented through several key releases and collaborations during that era.

---

## 1. Biography
Milá Melo is a composer, singer, and political militant who was highly active in the Angolan music scene during the 1970s. A native of Huambo, Angola, her catalog is deeply rooted in regional folklore. This central-Angolan heritage is reflected directly in her music, which features prominent tracks sung in Umbundu.

During the deeply fractured political landscape of post-independence Angola, Milá Melo was a militant for UNITA. This political alignment makes one of her most famous recordings a unique piece of history. She recorded the album _Utima Uetu (Folclore de Angola)_ on a record label that shared the name of the UNITA party. However, she was backed on this album by members of the legendary Angolan band [[Os Kiezos]], a group that predominantly recorded with artists who were sympathizers of the rival MPLA.

Her music reached beyond Angola's borders during the 1970s. She recorded an album for the São Vicente-based label _A Voz De Cabo Verde_, which was cut at Estúdio Musicorde in Portugal. On this record, which includes the track "Batuque," she was backed by the band Evolução África (consisting of Ze Moutinho, Nelo, João Rui, Zé Dias, and Victor).

In 1974, she collaborated with Conjunto Dipanda to record the Portuguese-language track "Quem Quer a Paz." Her catalog of local Angolan folklore includes well-known Umbundu tracks such as "Tuende Ke Nhala", "Tchakuparika", and "Mariquinha".

Following her active years in the Angolan music scene, her local recording career came to a halt. According to updates from listeners and archival channels, she eventually relocated and currently resides in France.
