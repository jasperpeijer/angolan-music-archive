# [[Tony Caetano]]

> **Core Summary:** Francisco "Tony" Caetano (1952–2018) was an Angolan musician and Kimbundu-language singer who helped define the country's urban popular music scene. Originally a percussionist, he transitioned to a solo career and achieved widespread acclaim in the 1970s and 1980s with enduring hits like [[Panguiami Uafua]] and [[Hungo]]. His musical contributions spanned over four decades, reflecting a lifelong dedication to Angolan culture that lasted until his final performances in the 2010s.

---

## 1. Biography
Francisco Caetano, better known as Tony Caetano was born in Luanda, Angola, in 1952. He began his musical journey in the 1970s, initially establishing himself as a percussionist for a local Luandan musical group.

### Solo Career and Musical Success
Eventually, Caetano transitioned to a solo career. He made his debut on "Cutonoca," a popular cultural and musical program hosted by the late presenter Luís Montez. Singing prominently in the Kimbundu language, he became a recognized figure in Angolan urban popular music. He released music through the Rebita label in Angola around 1973. Caetano authored several major hits that enjoyed widespread success throughout the 1970s and 1980s, most notably [[Panguiami Uafua]] and [[Hungo]]. In 2001, his music reached a broader audience when he was featured on the _Soul of Angola_ project, released by Teta Lando Productions in collaboration with Lusafrica.

### Death
Tony Caetano passed away at the age of 67 on July 25, 2018. He died at the Hospital Militar in Luanda following a prolonged illness.
