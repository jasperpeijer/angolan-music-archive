# [[Tony do Fumo]]

> **Core Summary:** António Filipe Manuel, known professionally as Tony do Fumo, was a foundational pillar of Angolan music who rose to prominence during the 1970s golden era of Angolan music. Recording heavily in Kimbundu, he became synonymous with the Semba and Rebita genres through defining collaborations with legendary backing bands like [[Os Kiezos]] and [[Os Jovens do Prenda]]. Though he has since passed, his cultural legacy is actively maintained and performed today by his son, Tony do Fumo Filho.

---

## 1. Biography
Born António Filipe Manuel on October 10, 1950, in Bairro Prenda, Luanda, Angola, he became widely known by his artistic aliases Tony do Fumo.

### Musical Career
Tony do Fumo emerged as a foundational pillar and one of the greatest interpreters of Angolan Urban Music (_Música Urbana Angolana_), becoming heavily associated with traditional styles like Semba and Rebita. His active recording career took off in the 1970s, a golden era for Angolan music. During this period, he notably recorded for the historic Angolan label **Rebita**.

His discography frequently utilized both the Kimbundu and Portuguese languages. This cross-cultural linguistic approach is perfectly exemplified by his track [[Monami (Tony do Fumo)|Monami]], a Kimbundu-language song released on a 1970s Rebita 7" vinyl single, on which he was backed by the legendary Angolan ensemble [[Os Kiezos]].

### Later Life & Legacy
Tony do Fumo passed away in 1991, though the exact date and location of his death remain undocumented in primary available public archives. His passing is explicitly verified by contemporary press, such as a 2018 interview in _OPaís_ with his son, Tony do Fumo Filho. His son continues his legacy today by performing the music of his father and attempting to release his unarchived tracks for modern audiences.

## 3. Network & Collaborators
* **[[Os Kiezos]]:** One of the most influential ensembles in Angolan musical history, they served as a primary backing band for much of Tony do Fumo's catalog. Beyond backing him on the track [[Monami]], historical tracklists from his comprehensive 2009 _Memorias_ compilation confirm Os Kiezos backed him on numerous other major recordings, including "Sul Africano," "Rumba Kalumba," and "Lamento De Mingo".
* **[[Os Jovens do Prenda]]:** Another foundational Angolan group that frequently collaborated with Tony do Fumo as his backing band. Archival vinyl data and compilation tracklists confirm Os Jovens do Prenda provided the instrumentation for some of his biggest 1974 releases, including the tracks [[Gingololo]], [[Zinha Peneirenta]], [[Marica]] and [[A Barona]].
* **[[Tony do Fumo Filho]]:** While not a collaborator during his father's active recording peak, his son serves as his primary legacy network today. As confirmed by the 2018 _OPaís_ interview, his son actively performs his catalog and works to release the unarchived, unpublished tracks left behind by his father.
