# Pobreza em Angola: efeito da guerra, efeitos da paz

## 1. Institutions & Strategic Frameworks Linked
*   **MINARS:** Ministério da Assistência e Reinserção Social
*   **Estratégia de Combate à Pobreza (2005):** The Angolan government's poverty reduction strategy which identified rural poverty as deeply affected by the war.
*   **Programa de Governo 2009-2013:** A national strategy document focusing on growth, diversification, and combating hunger and misery while reducing poverty.
*   **IBEP (Inquérito Integrado Sobre o Bem-estar da População):** A 2008-2009 integrated survey on population well-being conducted by the Instituto Nacional de Estatística.
*   **IDR & MICS:** Inquérito de Despesas e Receitas (2001) and Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (2001), used to estimate the Human Poverty Index.

## 2. Conceptual Glossary
*   **Monetary Approach to Poverty:** Defines poverty primarily as a lack of consumption or income relative to a defined poverty line.
*   **Capabilities Approach:** Focuses on deprivation regarding minimum or basic capabilities, following the work of Amartya Sen.
*   **Social Exclusion Approach:** Analyzes poverty through the lens of socially defined processes of marginalization and deprivation.
*   **Participatory Approach:** Operates on the premise that poverty should be defined by the individuals experiencing it, rather than externally.

## 3. Extracted Context & Clipped Texts

### A. The War's Direct Impact on Poverty
> *"Décadas sucessivas de conflito militar provocaram em Angola, não apenas perdas substanciais ao nível do capital físico (equipamentos, infra-estruturas, habitações) e humano, mas também determinaram efeitos que se combinaram entre si no sentido de uma redução do bem-estar da generalidade dos angolanos"*

* **Data Extracted:** The successive decades of military conflict in Angola caused substantial losses in physical capital and human capital. The war also resulted in compulsory displacement, food insecurity, malnutrition, and severe difficulties in accessing health and education. Furthermore, the conflict limited population access to cultivation areas and markets, destroyed peasant resources, and triggered a massive influx of displaced people into cities.

### B. The Correlation Between Peace and Poverty
> *"O fim da guerra salva vidas - incluindo as dos pobres que são frequentemente as principais vítimas - mas pode não trazer muita se alguma melhoria às condições de vida"*

* **Data Extracted:** Peace itself is not a direct factor in reducing poverty. While the end of the war saves lives, it does not automatically guarantee significant improvements in living conditions. The reciprocal relationship indicates that war retards development, but development also retards war. 

### C. Shifting Government Priorities Post-War
* **Data Extracted:** In the sector of social assistance and reintegration, there was a clear transition from a focus on food aid (which lasted until 2003) to an approach targeting the most vulnerable by 2004. By 2005, humanitarian aid strictly associated with the war was ceased. Since 2008, the MINARS programs have largely focused on populations directly affected by poverty, such as vulnerable families and the elderly.

## 4. Key Demographic & Statistical Extracts (2000 vs. 2009)

### The 2000 Baseline (Based on IDR)
*   The Human Poverty Index in Angola was estimated at 0.41 in the year 2000.
*   It was estimated that 68% of the population was poor, meaning they had access to less than 1.7 dollars a day.
*   It was estimated that 28% lived in conditions of extreme poverty, surviving on less than 0.76 dollars a day.

### The 2009 IBEP Findings
*   **National Poverty:** The incidence of poverty (population living below the national poverty line) dropped to 36.6%. 
*   **Urban vs. Rural:** The incidence of poverty was 58.3% in rural areas compared to 18.7% in urban areas.
*   **Education:** 29% of Angolan children had to travel more than 2 km daily to attend school. 26% of children aged 6 to 9 had never attended school. The national illiteracy rate was 34%, with severe disadvantages for women (nearly 50% illiterate) and the rural population (over 70% illiterate).
*   **Infrastructure:** Only 42% of the population used an appropriate source of drinking water. Only 53% of households had some type of sanitary installation at home. Nationally, 88% of households lived in inadequate housing, reaching 99% in rural areas.
*   **Health:** In rural areas, only 24% of the population had access to public health posts or centers within a 2 km radius, compared to 63% in urban areas.
*   **Migration:** Internal migration remained high, with 12.6% of the population identified as internal migrants in 2009.
