# Bridling and Marriage (Alambamento e Casamento)

## 1. Etymology & Definition

### The Meaning of Alambamento
* The term *alambamento* derives from the Kimbundu word *kulemba*, which translates to paying tribute to the future father-in-law through conventional gifts.
* The word took its current form when the Kimbundu infinitive radical "Ku" was replaced due to the linguistic influence of Portuguese.
* It is a traditional Angolan ceremony that occurs when a couple decides to live together, marking the second phase of the union after the groom is presented to the bride's family.
* It is often considered by Luandan society to be more culturally significant than formal civil or religious marriage.

### Cultural Misinterpretations
* The Portuguese complementary dictionary by Maia (1994) erroneously assigns the term as a synonym for a "dowry" or "bride price".
* The translation of the concept into a commercial deed of purchase and sale was the result of a Portuguese colonial ideology aimed at distorting the native culture.

## 2. The Ritual & "The List"

### Preparation and Compensation
*   The bride's uncles are responsible for setting the date of the request, as they play a critical role in the bride's education.
*   The uncles present the groom with a list of goods that must be gathered and offered to the bride's family.
*   These goods act as compensation for the expenses the family incurred while raising the bride from birth.
*   A highly valued payment increases the social prestige of the bride.

### Items on the List
*   An envelope containing money, typically stipulated by the uncle between 300 and 500 USD.
*   The bride's physical height measured in stacked beer cans.
*   The bride's physical height measured in pallets of juice or Coca-Cola.
*   A goat, a suit for the uncle, and a pair of shoes for the mother.
*   If the bride is already pregnant—a situation described as the groom having "jumped out the window"—the required quantities on the list are heavily increased.

### The Ceremony
*   The groom's family travels to the bride's home, where her uncle acts as a judge for the proceedings.
*   A formal letter requesting the bride's hand is read, replacing the older tradition of using human emissaries.
*   Once the father agrees and the goods are verified, the official wedding date is set, followed by a party with abundant food, drink, and traditional music.

## 3. Extracted Historical Context & Modern Challenges

### The Impact of Portuguese Colonialism
*   Portuguese colonizers aimed to alienate African populations by converting them to Christianity and imposing Western standards of "civilization".
*   Catholic figures like Father Francisco Valente actively condemned the alambamento in writing, labeling it an embarrassing habit that promoted the inequality of African women.
*   This ideology successfully divided Angolan society, deeply affecting the "assimilated" class who abandoned their traditions to align with Portuguese cultural standards.

### The Shift in Traditions (1951-1970)
*   During this period, several rules of Bantu marriage changed or vanished in Luanda.
*   The public proof of virginity, which used to be displayed via a blood-stained handkerchief or checked using a dove's egg or a candle wick, lost its societal value.
*   The rite of passage called "mussula" which marked a woman's transition into motherhood, was annulled.
*   The inclusion of a wine jug was added to the ritual proceedings.

### Modernity and The Civil War (1992-2002)
*   The prolonged Angolan civil war generated extreme poverty and destabilized the extended family structures in Luanda.
*   Many young people now view the alambamento as an outdated ritual belonging to countryside populations and avoid it due to a lack of financial means.
*   While living together without performing the alambamento traditionally resulted in a lack of societal respect, the practice of skipping it is becoming increasingly common due to modern socio-economic pressures.
