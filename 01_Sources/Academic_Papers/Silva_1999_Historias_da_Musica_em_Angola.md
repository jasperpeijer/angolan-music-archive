# Histórias da Música em Angola

## 1. Entities Linked & Key Biographical Extracts

### Foundational Figures & 19th/Early 20th Century Composers
*   [[Augusto Tadeu Bastos]] (1872/1936): Born in Benguela, a multifaceted intellectual and composer who wrote the waltz "As Furnas do Lobito".
*   [[Alfredo Troni]] (1845/1904): A famous Portuguese lawyer who frequented Masemba circles so often he was immortalized in a local song.
*   [[Cordeiro da Matta]] (1857/1894): Published a foundational book of proverbs and riddles in 1891 that inspired later popular music.
*   [[Luís Gomes Sambo]] (1874/1946): A prominent figure from Cabinda who dedicated himself to classical music, parading his fanfare band through the streets of Luanda and Kaxitu.
*   [[António de Assis Júnior]] (1877/1960): The first president of the African National League in 1930 and author of a Kimbundu-Portuguese dictionary.
*   [[Voto Neves]]: Former treasurer of Luanda City Hall and a guitar player who noted the melodic similarities between Angolan music and the Brazilian *baião*.

### Key Figures of the Musseques & Early Ensembles
*   [[António Palma]]: Originating from Kabiri, he was a Justice official who played plucked string instruments, died in exile in Lubangu, and is the father of the famous 1960s singer [[Belita Palma]].
*   [[Abel Fontes Pereira]]: A top accordionist and the father of the great composer Fontinhas.
*   [[Filipe Amado]] (Nickname: **Karola**) (1894/1968): An accordion and dikanza player who commanded semba circles as a master of ceremonies.
*   [[Manuel dos Passos]]: A master of *kimbandismo* and intellectual raised by the powerful mestiza spiritist [[Xika Aniceto]] (1872/1959).
*   [[Álvaro Caldas]]: A highly popular figure deeply embedded in the Bairro Operário who preferred the poor, carnival salons over elite bourgeois clubs.
*   [[Flávio Galiano]]: An athletic bank official who played tambourine for the "Coração" dance group and was known for fighting to defend his carnival hosts.

### The 1940s-1960s Generation
*  [[Liceu Vieira Dias]]: A central figure who learned guitar from [[Óscar Araújo]] (one of the OSMACE brothers), formed the *Grupo dos Sambas*, and later helped transition Angolan music from Brazilian styles back to Kimbundu roots with [[N'gola Ritmos]].
*  [[Ana Maria Mascarenhas]]: Revealed as an excellent composer in 1952, whose songs were later recorded by her sister, Conchinha.
*  [[Quim Jorge]]: Replaced Fontes Pereira in the Ngongo Theater Group and proved to be an excellent composer.
*   [[Eleutério Sanches]]: A poet, musician, and painter who authored the famously simple and unforgettable melody "Luanda".
*   [[Lilly Tchiumba]] (1937/1989): Considered an artist-combatant and pioneer in fighting for the rights of female artists.

## 2. Kimbundu & Cultural Glossary

### Musical and Spiritual Terms
*   **Masemba:** Navel bumps; the plural of *semba* and a highly popular dance in Luanda.
*   **Kilundu:** A spirit or being of the invisible world (diminutive: *Kalundu*).
*   **Samba:** A prayer; the infinitive of the verb *Kusamba* (to pray).
*   **Tangu:** Branch (possible origin of the word Tango).
*   **Milonga:** Plural of *mulonga*, meaning crime, mystery, offense, figurative, or resentment.
*   **Jisabu:** Proverbs.
*   **Jinongo-nongo:** Riddles.

### Narrative and Everyday Vocabulary
*   **Madya Kandimba:** Maria Bunny (Maria Coelhinho). Name of a famous song about a European man falling in love with his African maid.
*   **Kinjangu:** Can mean the name of an Italian person (Quinjango) or translate to a machete wielder / big machete
*   **Njangu:** Machete (Plural: *Jinjangu*).
*   **Hanga:** Wild guineafowl (galinha do mato).
*   **Sanji:** Domestic chicken (galinha-de-casa).
*   **Kisweya:** Tyrant or beast.

### Culinary Terms
*   **Mwamba wa sanji**, **Mwamba wa mbiji**, **Funji ya xitu**, **Funji ya mbiji**: Traditional delicacies made during Saturday musical gatherings.
*   **Muzongê:** A fish broth with palm oil consumed at dawn to cure hangovers.

## 3. Traditional Instruments Catalog

### Idiophones (Percussion by themselves)
*   **Saxi (katxakatxa):** A maraca made of *maboque* filled with dry seeds or beads.
*   **Bavugu:** A Kung group instrument made of three greased gourds played by compressing and decompressing air against the thigh.
*   **Marimba:** An instrument of Asian cultural influence found near the Kalandula falls and Lunda region.
*   **Kissanji / Mbwetete:** A lamellophone with bamboo or metal keys plucked by the thumbs, used for storytelling or long walks (called *Mbwetete* when made of bamboo).

### Membranophones (Drums)
*   **Ngoma (Batuque / Tam-tam):** Historically used to send messages, with various regional models.
*   **Mpwita:** A friction drum originating from the Kongo, played with a wet hand rubbing an internal wooden stick.

### Aerophones (Wind)
*   **Mpungu:** A horn of Kikongo origin.
*   **Vandumbu:** A soft wood horn wrapped in fibers, from the Ambwela group.
*   **Njembo-erose:** An antelope horn with a beeswax resonator used by Himba shepherds.

### Chordophones (Strings)
*   **Hungu (Mbulumbumba):** A struck string instrument made from a bow and gourd, taken to Brazil where it became known as the *berimbau*.
*   **Kakocha:** A type of three-stringed violin (bowed).
*   **Tchihumba:** A plucked instrument with five or more *mateba* strings and a resonance box.

## 4. Dances Mentioned
*   **Angolan Origin/Practice:** Kabetula, Kilaphanga, Semba (Rebita), Makinu, Kaduke, Masemba, Coração, Pincão.
*   **Diaspora/Foreign Influences:** Fandango, Fofa, Lundu, Samba, Tango, Milonga, Blues, Jazz, Merengue, Cotillon (French).

## 5. Extracted Historical Context

### A. The Story of "Madya Kandimba" (1875)
*   **Data Extracted:** Composed around 1875, it is one of the earliest collected pieces of the Masemba choir. 
*   **Data Extracted:** The song tells the narrative of a European man who falls in love with his African maid. 
*   **Data Extracted:** When the European's wife discovers the romance, she searches for the maid with a pistol, prompting the maid to flee by boat.

### B. The Etymological Link Between Semba, Samba, and Kusamba
*   **Data Extracted:** The rhythm known as *Kaduke* in Angola transformed into *Samba* when taken to Brazil.
*   **Data Extracted:** Liceu Vieira Dias argued that *Samba* is not a folkloric term, but rather derived from the Kimbundu infinitive *Kusamba*, which means "to pray".
*   **Data Extracted:** Enslaved Africans gathered in the yards of Brazilian plantations at night to pray to the gods for a return to their homeland, using the term *Samba*.
*   **Data Extracted:** Plantation owners misunderstood this religious, ethno-choreographic manifestation as a purely recreational and festive act, permanently linking the religious prayer to a secular festival.
