# Ngulungo by [[Banda Movimento]] & [[Day Doy]]

> **Thematic Core:** *Ngulungo* by Banda Movimento is a lively _semba_ track, in which singer Day Doy pokes fun at what is likely an old, exaggerated urban legend about the Angolan municipality of Golungo Alto. The song tells the story of a father who went to his grave vowing never to visit the region, terrified by a false rumor that the locals would cook people whenever they ran out of pork. Ultimately, the singer brushes the myth off as a complete lie ("makutu me"), turning a dark historical rumor into a catchy tribute to the town.
---

## 1. Context & Linguistic Significance
*Ngulungo* deals directly with the power of a local urban legend. The song outlines a very specific, dark rumor: that the people of Golungo Alto would scorch and eat humans when they ran out of pork. The text highlights how deeply this rumor was believed by showing two extremes: a father who took a vow to his grave to never step foot there, and white people (_mindele_) who were too afraid to visit. The singer uses the song to explicitly debunk the myth, plainly calling it a lie (_"makutu me"_).

### Linguistic Notes
* **Mindele:** This is the plural of _mundele_, the standard Kimbundu term for a white person. Its inclusion shows the rumor was widespread enough to cross cultural boundaries and frighten away outsiders.
* **Swaaa!:** This ideophone or onomatopoeia is characteristic of Day Doy's music.
