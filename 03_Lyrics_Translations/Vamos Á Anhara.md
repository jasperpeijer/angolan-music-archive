# Vamos Á Anhara (Tuende Ke Nhala) by [[Milá Melo]]

> **Thematic Core:** *Tuende Ke Nhala* or *Vamos Á Anhara* by Milá Melo is a highly repetitive, straightforward track. The lyrics consist entirely of a grandmother stating they are going to the savanna (_chana_), followed by the observation that it is cold there and they are going to shiver.

---

## 1. Context & Linguistic Significance
Because the song only loops two basic statements, there is no extended narrative. It functions as a simple, literal chant about going to a cold environment. Any deeper cultural or historical meaning is not present in the text itself.

### Linguistic Context
#todo/verify-fact 
* **Agglutination in _Tukaluluma_:** This word demonstrates how Umbundu builds sentences by stacking prefixes on a verb root. **Tu-** (we) + **-ka-** (future tense) + **-luluma** (shiver) forms the complete thought "we will shiver."
* **Locative Prefixes:** Umbundu uses prefixes to change spatial meaning. The root word _enhala_ is modified to **kwenhala** to mean "to the savanna" (direction), and **kenhala** to mean "in the savanna" (location).
* **The Translation of _Chana_:** Translating _enhala_ to the Portuguese word _chana_ is a direct Angolanism. European Portuguese does not use this word. The word is used to describe a savanna.
