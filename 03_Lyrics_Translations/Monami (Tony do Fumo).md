# Monami by [[Tony do Fumo]]

> **Thematic Core:** *Monami* by Tony de Fumo is a heartfelt dialogue from an overwhelmed father trying to comfort his crying child by reminding them that he has provided everything they could ever want. He expresses a deep love and a desire to pass down his profession of singing, while gently pleading for a moment of peace because his mind is heavily burdened with personal worries.

---

## 1. Context & Linguistic Significance
"Monami" steps away from the broader social or political themes often found in Angolan music of the era to focus on a deeply intimate, universal domestic moment: a father trying to soothe a restless child. The song shifts between two distinct emotional states. First, the father uses both material reassurance ("I bought everything you wanted") and deep affection ("I want to teach you my profession") to comfort the child. However, the tone shifts into raw exhaustion at the end, where he begs the child to stop crying and leave him in peace because he is dealing with his own heavy, unspoken burdens.

### Linguistic Notes
#todo/verify-fact 
* **Endearment (_Tata_):** The word _tata_ strictly means "father." However, in the line _"Monami tata"_ (Oh my child), the father is using it to address his son. This is a common linguistic feature in several African language families where a parent affectionately addresses a child using their own title (or an elder's title) as a profound term of endearment and respect.
* **Possessive Suffixes (_Monami_):** Kimbundu frequently attaches possession directly to the noun. _Mona_ (child) becomes _Monami_ (my child).
