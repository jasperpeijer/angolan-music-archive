# Ueji Ki Usokana by [[Dom Caetano]]

> **Thematic Core:** _Ueji Ki Usokana_ is a cautionary tragedy about the fatal consequences of unstable marriages. It tries to tell the listener that it is safer to marry a widow than a divorcee, because a widow's past cannot return to claim her. The singer does this by recounting a specific neighborhood dispute that ended in death when a wife abandoned her new husband for her ex.
---

## 1. Context & Linguistic Significance
_Ueji Ki Usokana_ acts as a warning about marriage. Dom Caetano uses an old Angolan proverb, which says that it is better to marry a widow than a divorcee ([[Angola24Horas_2018_Dom_Caetano_Contra|Angola24Horas, 2018]]). He compares the widow to a "wounded antelope" (she carries the pain of losing a spouse, but her past is buried). A divorcee, however, might just get angry and go back to her ex-husband.

To make his point, Dom Caetano seems to tell a story about his relative. The guy was stable and had a house (_kubata_), but he married a divorcee. Just as the warning goes, she got mad and went back to her ex. This sparked a massive dispute between the two men that ended in a fight, leaving one of Caetano's friends dead and buried (_mox'amavu_).

The final verses describe the chaos immediately following the murder. Dona Maria is chasing the culprits away, and the community is frantically looking for a woman named Minga so she can explain exactly what happened. The track ends with Caetano simply stating that he is going to catch the killers himself (_kwatayu_).
