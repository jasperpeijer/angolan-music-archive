# Avó Tete by [[Jivago]]

> **Thematic Core:** "Ramiro" functions as a raw _crónica social_, moving beyond the lighthearted satire of other 1980s tracks to document the volatile intersection of domestic violence and urban suspicion. The song acts as a direct reportage of a marital breakdown where the protagonist's defense against accusations of infidelity highlights the erosion of community trust—a persistent theme in Luanda’s rapidly changing social landscape where "peace has not reigned for a long time".

---

## 1. Context
The song sits at the heart of the 1980s musical pivot in Angola, where the focus shifted away from the ideological, state-sanctioned and politically tinted music of the independence era. By grounding the song in the specificities of an _alambamento_ (the traditional engagement ceremony that the protagonist now regrets), Jivago links the couple's private failure to the broader cultural tension between tradition and modern urban instability. The shift toward this genre reflects the period's social realism, where the song serves as a medium for publicizing, and thus mitigating, private trauma ([[Alves_2016_Oh_Muxima|Alves, 2016]]) ([[Alves_2013_Musicalidade_Politica|Alves, 2013]]) ([[Silva_1999_Historias_da_Musica_em_Angola|Silva, 1999]]).
