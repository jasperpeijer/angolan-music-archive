# The Angolan Music & Cultural Archive

## The Mission
This is an open-source, relational archive dedicated to the preservation of Angolan music history. It focuses heavily on the linguistic and social evolution of genres like Semba, Kizomba, and Kuduro. The ultimate goal is to also apply this to other Angolan and regional dance and music styles, such as Tchianda, Kabetula, Kilapanga, Rebita, Congolese Rumba, etc.

My ultimate goal is to make a selfless contribution to the preservation of this history, ensuring that the origins, Kimbundu lyrics, and translated metaphors of Angolan music remain freely accessible to the public, researchers, and the diaspora.

## How the Archive is Structured
This repository is built as a relational Markdown database (designed for Obsidian):
* **/01_Sources:** Summaries, citations, and notes extracted from academic papers, historical books, and interviews.
* **/02_Core_Entities:** Biographical profiles of artists, histories of specific eras, and the evolution of musical genres.
* **/03_Lyrics_Translations:** Side-by-side linguistic analyses of original tracks, focusing heavily on Kimbundu/Portuguese code-switching and cultural metaphors.
* **/04_Papers:** PDFs of downloaded papers and articles that have been used in this archive as sources.

## Using this Data
This archive is built in public. You are free to browse the Markdown files, read the lyric translations, and utilize the historical timelines for your own research. Every artist profile and lyric sheet is rigorously cross-referenced with academic and historical sources located in the `01_Sources` folder.

## Contributing: Help Build the Archive
This archive is a living, open-source project. Angolan music history—especially from the colonial and civil war eras—is frequently undocumented, lost, or scattered across oral histories and out-of-print vinyl records. No single person can archive it all. 

I am actively looking for input, corrections, and collaboration from the community. Your contribution is highly valued if you can help with:

* **Linguistic Translation:** Correcting and translating Kimbundu, Umbundu, Chokwe, Lingala or Kikongo lyrics, or explaining historical slang and metaphors.
* **Transcription:** Transcribing the lyrics of songs in any of the abovementioned languages, and/or Portuguese and French.
* **Historical Fact-Checking:** Verifying recording dates, band lineups, or providing context that isn't found in academic papers.
* **Lost Media:** Providing liner notes, photos, or audio files from rare vinyl pressings (like early *N'Gola* or *CDA* labels).
* **Artist Biographies:** Fleshing out the profiles of lesser-known or unknown artists who defined an era but missed the historical spotlight.

## Disclaimer on AI Usage

During the development of this music archive, AI has been used for the following purposes:
- Translation from Portuguese to English
- Writing summaries of songs, artists, etc.
- Finding articles, papers, and other sources
- Adding context and annotations to lyrics (including translation of single words from Kimbundu and other languages)

While some of these actions are highly sensitive to mistakes (especially translation of low-resource languages like Kimbundu), they are always fact checked by a real person and supplied with an actual source where necessary. Whenever a proper source can't be found, or won't support a statement, but still seems highly plausible, a #todo/verify-fact tag is added to the piece of information.

This way AI is used as a tool to decrease workload, as this is currently a single-person project. At the same time it is not used as a source of truth, keeping hallucinations and inconsistencies out of the archive.

While I try my best to keep any mistakes or inconsistencies out of the archive, I currently do not speak any of the African languages that are used in semba and kizomba music, meaning I rely on the Kimbundu (or other language) to Portuguese translations provided by external sources. While I try my best to have a critical look at them and keep mistakes out, I am often not able to identify mistakes in these sources, nor can I verify whether AI was used for these sources.
